# NaiNai: two-minute quote-only demo

Current October 24, 2026. Plan only; no supplier contacted, product implemented or real quotation obtained. Canonical scope: [quote-only MVP](superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-nainai-quote-only-mvp-design.md). Reliability gates and unexecuted checks: [acceptance/readiness matrix](NAINAI_RELIABILITY.md).

## One story

**“NaiNai turns a buying brief into a quote you can inspect, negotiate and review before committing.”**

Two cases, one product: the user's real personal table need and an explicitly evaluation-only compute scenario for potential AI-company use. The finish line is a reviewed quote/findings packet. No order, purchase, deposit, contract acceptance, financial/provider reservation or GPU instance is created.

## Preload before judging

- Table request: T318-1, quantity one, < $150 including delivery/tax/fees, Decatur 30033. Keep the full address private. New and inspected floor/open-box alternatives remain distinct.
- Actual table outcome and original written quotation if obtained. Preserve refusal, no response, missing costs and unchanged/over-budget results. Never replace them with a fabricated deal.
- A 10-15 second permitted excerpt from the working system's real supplier interaction; visible recording label/date. If audio permission is unavailable, show separately permitted written evidence or an allowed transcript/summary.
- Compute case: persistent **Evaluation only — no actual workload or purchase plan** badge. Show lead-chosen illustrative assumptions separately from market observations and any authentic provider response.
- One isolated synthetic changed-term/minimum-commitment case with its own banner and IDs. Test actions never edit original supplier evidence.
- Case switcher, global outreach pause, conversation takeover/stop and review/export controls. No purchasing controls. No vendor must answer during judging.

## Reviewer-visible engineering

Keep human authority, immutable quote revisions, provenance, costs/unknowns, refused terms and pause/stop visible. Demonstrate one meaningful stale-review or missing-cost check live. Repeat review returns one record for that revision and creates no commercial side effects. A saved packet preserves failures; “reviewed” does not mean “qualified” or “accepted.”

Reliability revision leaves the timed script unchanged. Reopening a saved workspace must not start a call. Show provider failure, unresolved delivery, cached-source timestamp and stop-pending acknowledgement as their actual states; never replace a live failure with an unlabeled simulation. The isolated synthetic test workspace cannot send external actions. Any real call is arranged before judging with separate authority; the backup uses permitted saved evidence with explicit recorded/cached labels. The newer receipt/next-step/challenge enhancements remain deferred.

Example-only minimum-term answer key: eight GPUs x 72 hours x $3/GPU-hour = $1,728 compute charge; eight GPUs x 168-hour minimum x $2 = $2,688. Rates are fictional, fees/tax are not included in those two numbers, and the longer term is a separate scope option. Missing extras prevent a complete-cost claim. Do not call the difference money saved.

## Market/adoption Q&A

Use dated market context in the appendix, not a long opening slide: NVIDIA reported $89.0 billion Data Center revenue, +117% year over year, for the quarter ended July 26, 2026 ([August 26 primary release](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-second-quarter-fiscal-2027)). This supports discussion of infrastructure-market growth; it does not prove universal GPU scarcity, a particular provider's availability, demand for NaiNai, or a real buyer behind the example.

Say plainly: the table need is real; AI-company applicability remains a hypothesis. Shadeform and ComputePrices already offer sourcing/agent/data capabilities. NaiNai's proposed contribution is its buyer-controlled cross-category negotiation/evidence workflow; show the actual differences before claiming superiority. Two playbooks demonstrate transfer, not universal buying expertise.
