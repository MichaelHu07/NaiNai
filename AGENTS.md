# NaiNai workspace instructions

## Objective and sources

Build a strong solo Cal Hacks submission with maximum credible track coverage through NaiNai, a small-business buying team. The user selected this direction over OpenBooks. The core journey is request -> sourced offer comparison -> separately authorized negotiation -> human review -> saved quote/findings packet. Two bounded cases share one workflow: the coffee table is a real personal need; cloud GPU compute is evaluation-only, with no actual workload/company buyer. Keep a second submission deferred until the first is complete.

Read HACK.md, docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-23-nainai-quote-only-mvp-design.md and docs/NAINAI_DEMO.md for current scope/status. This quote-only revision supersedes both earlier designs and their purchase-approval/budget-reservation endpoint; those files remain historical references. CAL_HACKS_13_TRACKS.md supplies requirements and sources. OPENBOOKS_STRATEGY.md and OPENBOOKS_WORKFLOW.md are historical references. Organizer/sponsor requirements outrank third-party advice. Product implementation begins during the event; research/planning may precede it.

The current development plan is docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-23-nainai-quote-only.md. Use its shared interfaces, lead/three-worker ownership and reviewable milestones when implementation is requested; preserve intent/evidence labels, strict-cap and changed-condition semantics. No scaffold has been created.

Read OPENBOOKS_JUDGES.md for researched professional backgrounds and presentation preparation. Distinguish explicit event introductions, profile labels and colleague reports. Do not imply anyone is assigned to our table or predict personal judging preferences. Use documented expertise to prepare evidence and questions while keeping the published rubric authoritative.

## Operating rules

- Define the demo-visible outcome before expanding a feature or integration. Prioritize a complete path from request to genuine negotiated quote or honest unresolved outcome to nonbinding human review and saved findings.
- Keep one actual buyer, USD, two typed category playbooks, up to three concurrent public research jobs, up to 100 genuine observations, at most five separately approved distinct suppliers across both cases and one active external conversation globally. Two capabilities: find/negotiate suitable quotes; review evidence and save findings. No checkout, purchase/draft purchase request, offer/contract acceptance, financial or capacity reservation, deposit, payment, funded trial or instance launch exists in the MVP. Selected personal pilot: Kenny's Warehouse Steals Wickman Falls T318-1, advertised $177.14; target strictly under $150 all-in (14999 cents maximum), delivery in Decatur, GA 30033. Inspected floor/open-box alternatives may be considered with exact condition/inspection/warranty/acceptance evidence; changes from new are separate scope. Supplier contact remains separately unauthorized; scope selection or review is not permission to contact. Compute is evaluation-only: no fictional company, real-workload claim or buying commitment. Its illustrative configuration/ceiling is independent of the table cap. Keep the private street/apartment address out of repository files, public searches and demos.
- Evaluate changes against usefulness, intentionality, functionality, creativity and engineering depth. Track count is useful only when the implemented product genuinely meets the sponsor rule.
- Make the comparison/conversation/evidence/review experience available early using explicitly labeled fixtures, then integrate real services. Use an existing suitable component system; avoid custom design infrastructure.
- Demo entry may use an isolated synthetic workspace. Server-side authorization, revision-bound review validation and credential handling still apply.
- Show real progress, errors and evidence sources. Do not invent loading delays, model reasoning, API results, user research, sponsor approval, savings or measured outcomes.
- Distinguish live service calls, deterministic calculations, seeded company records and simulations in UI, docs and the presentation.
- Public product listings establish observations, not negotiated supplier commitments. Keep fictional supplier quote terms and sandbox tax labeled in every packet and audit trail. Preserve scenario intent separately from evidence origin; authentic indicative GPU terms do not establish a real workload. A real conversation with a human playing a supplier is still a simulated commercial counterparty.
- Real supplier negotiation is the primary demo target. Simulators support tests, not claims of real bargaining. Preserve authentic initial/final quotes and equivalent terms; distinguish negotiated quote reductions from paid/fulfilled savings. Do not invent competing offers, future orders, buyer identity, supplier confirmation or discounts. Respect recorded contact/AI/recording/sharing permissions, human takeover and the user's purchasing authority.
- Record evidence before claiming eligibility. A development tool does not count as a runtime integration unless the track explicitly allows that kind of usage.
- Prioritize five programs with evidence: Visa, Cartesia, Microsoft, Apify and Figma; defer Hyphenate because the quote-only MVP removes the finance world, purchase approval and reservations. A personal purchase is not company validation. Anthropic requires actual narrow-community validation; CREAO or Factory requires a verified central runtime role. Add only one technical stretch at a time after the baseline works. Drop unavailable or irrelevant integrations without misrepresenting eligibility.
- Use decimal-safe rates and integer-cent final totals, application-enforced permissions and review bound to current request/quote/evidence/permission versions with duplicate/concurrent-action protection. Changed revisions invalidate current review; preserve historical snapshots. Missing fees, tax or hard specifications prevent qualifying/complete-cost claims, but incomplete/no-deal findings may be reviewed/saved without changing their status. Review creates no commercial side effects. Test critical behavior rather than cosmetic implementation details.
- User validation must come from the human's lived experience or real participants. Agents may prepare interview materials and analyze provided notes, but must not fabricate people, quotes or interview outcomes.
- Do not contact mentors, sponsors, interviewees or other people without explicit user authorization. Prepare concise questions for the user to use.
- Prepare sponsor-specific evidence and Q&A using the actual rubric; keep product claims consistent across presentations.

## Subagents and integration

One lead plus at most three simultaneous workers in this session. Lead owns shared contracts, HACK.md, scope, integration and final evidence. Give each worker an exclusive module/file scope and acceptance criteria. Workers report changes, verification commands/results, failure modes, and demo-visible evidence.

Keep a UI worker active early. The lead handles contract/integration decisions while the human can validate the user problem and obtain necessary account access. Rotate a worker to reliability review and submission artifacts after the core path works. Preserve exact API shapes in shared contracts rather than having workers guess them independently.

Each completed task must state: what the user can now do; how it was checked; whether the demo script changed; which claimed track requirement is supported; and any real/seeded/simulated boundary.

## Scope review and finish

At each completed integration or meaningful work block:
1. Run or inspect both bounded cases through the shared quote-only scenario.
2. Record the weakest judging dimension and next action in HACK.md.
3. Check the user evidence and sponsor-evidence status.
4. Review any remaining integration against the demo and the time budget.

Proposed finish policy: stop optional features with 12 hours remaining; freeze optional integrations with 6 hours remaining. Continue core-critical repairs and required artifacts. The lead may tighten the cutoff when reliability or the real clock requires it. Never treat elapsed time as approval.

Before declaring the first submission ready: verify reproducibility, the critical scenario matrix, a saved Devpost draft, required links/artifacts, a backup recording, and three rehearsals of the short demo. Then consider whether a second distinct submission is allowed and feasible.
